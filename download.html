Installing the app

You install the app from a disk image and open it once with a right-click.

The first launch shows a Gatekeeper warning because the app is not yet notarized, and the right-click step clears it for good.

  • Download the latest disk image from GitHub Releases. Grab the most recent .dmg from the releases page and open it.
  • Drag the app into your Applications folder. Move Local Meeting Manager out of the disk image and into Applications the usual way.
  • Open it once with a right-click. Because the app is not yet notarized with an Apple Developer ID, right-click it and choose Open the first time, then confirm; you will not see the warning again.
  • Grant the permissions it asks for. Allow Microphone and Screen Recording so it can capture the call, and optionally Calendar, Reminders, and Contacts for prep and follow-ups.
  • Record your first meeting. Start a call, let the app capture it, and read the summary it writes on your Mac when you stop.
Requirements

Local Meeting Manager runs on a modern Mac and asks only for the permissions it uses.

What you need to run it

The app leans on Apple Silicon for transcription and the local model.

  • It runs on macOS 14.4 or newer.
  • Apple Silicon is strongly recommended, because transcription and the local model lean on the Neural Engine.
  • Intel Macs work with the smaller transcription models.
  • The WhisperKit model is about 1.5 GB and downloads once, while recordings and the database grow with use.

The permissions it asks for, and why

Each request maps to something the app actually does.

  • Microphone lets the app record your own voice.
  • Screen Recording lets it capture system audio and read the call-window title.
  • Calendar lets it show your meetings and the people attending them.
  • Reminders and Contacts are optional, for pushing action items and importing names.

Updates are free, and you download them yourself.

The app does not auto-update and makes no update-check calls. When a new version ships, you download it from the same GitHub Releases page and replace the app, and your data stays exactly where it is. Every update is included in the one-time price.

Your data lives in one folder you can back up.

Local Meeting Manager keeps a single SQLite database and an audio folder under ~/Library/Application Support/, and your API keys live in the macOS Keychain rather than in a file. Backing everything up is as simple as copying that folder to another disk.
