Your meeting audio, transcripts, and voice profiles stay on your Mac by default.

Local Meeting Manager treats meeting content as sensitive from the first recording. Here is exactly what stays on your Mac, what leaves only when you switch it on, and what the app never does.

What always stays on your Mac

These never leave your device, whatever AI option you pick.

  • Your audio recordings, the .wav files, stay in a local folder.
  • Your transcripts stay local, because speech recognition runs on your Mac.
  • The voice fingerprints used for speaker identification stay on the device.
  • Your notes, calendar data, and the people directory stay local.
  • The knowledge-base index of your own documents stays on your Mac.

What leaves only when you turn it on

Each of these is off until you enable it, and you choose what is shared.

  • Cloud AI sends the relevant transcript slice and your prompt to Anthropic, but only if you add a Claude API key.
  • Local AI sends nothing off the machine, because the model runs over localhost.
  • Google Calendar gives the app read-only access to the events you authorize.
  • Apple Calendar and Contacts are read-only, and Reminders is written to only for the items you push.
  • Apollo.io sends an attendee's email address to look up a title and employer, and never any meeting content.

What the app never does

These are not settings to find; the app simply does not do them.

  • It sends no telemetry, with no analytics SDK and no usage tracking.
  • It makes no update-check calls, because updates are manual downloads.
  • It creates no account, because there is no Local Meeting Manager server.
  • It never uploads your raw audio or your voice fingerprints to anyone.

Cloud AI leaves your Mac only when you add a key, and only the text it needs.

When you add a Claude API key and trigger a summary, an attribution pass, a brief, a follow-up email, action-item extraction, or chat, the app sends the relevant slice of the cleaned transcript plus your prompt to Anthropic over HTTPS. Your raw audio and your voice fingerprints are never part of that request. If you would rather send nothing at all, the same features run against a local model over localhost, so the content never leaves the machine.

You can verify the network behavior yourself.

You do not have to take this on trust. With the app open, run the command below in Terminal and you will see only the AI and calendar providers you configured, and nothing else.

sudo lsof -i -P -n | grep -i meeting

Your data lives in one place that you can back up by copying a folder.

Local Meeting Manager keeps a single SQLite database and an audio folder under ~/Library/Application Support/ on your Mac, and any API keys live in the macOS Keychain rather than in a file. There is no companion service and no cloud account, so backing everything up is as simple as copying that folder. Because the record lives on your machine, there is no server that could be switched off and take your meetings with it.

You can run Local Meeting Manager fully offline with the local model, so recording, transcription, and AI summaries can all happen without an internet connection.
