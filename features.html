Local Meeting Manager covers the whole arc of a meeting, from prep to recall.

Every feature below runs from your own Mac and stays grounded in the meetings you have actually recorded. The capabilities are grouped the way you would use them, from capturing the call to asking questions about it months later.

Recording and capture

Local Meeting Manager starts recording the way that fits the moment and captures both sides of the call from your Mac.

  • It offers three ways to start. Recording begins automatically when a Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, or FaceTime window opens, from a calendar event, or as an ad-hoc meeting that records immediately.
  • It reads the window title, not your screen. Detection checks the active window title about once a second, so no screen content is captured just to decide when to record.
  • It captures both audio streams. Your microphone and the system audio from everyone else are recorded and mixed, so the transcript has every voice.
  • It handles real-world microphones. The app finds a working input across closed-lid, external-mic, and Bluetooth setups instead of getting stuck on a busy or silent default.
  • It keeps a live notepad during the call. You can type freely while recording, and your notes auto-save on every keystroke so they survive a crash.
  • It lets you reopen a meeting. If you stopped early or your Mac crashed, you can reopen the meeting, append more audio, and it merges into the existing transcript.
On-device transcription

Every recording is transcribed on your Mac with WhisperKit, at no per-meeting cost.

  • Speech recognition runs on-device. Nothing is uploaded to be transcribed, and there is no per-meeting transcription fee.
  • You can choose the model quality. The app defaults to large-v3-turbo and lets you step down to a smaller model when you want speed over fine detail.
  • It falls back rather than fails. If the primary model cannot load, Apple's built-in speech recognition engages automatically so transcription still completes.
  • The model downloads once. The first meeting pulls about 1.5 GB, and every meeting after that starts instantly.
Speaker identification

Speaker identification learns each voice once and recognizes it in every later meeting.

  • It combines five signals. Manual renames, voice fingerprints, spoken-name cues, elimination, and an AI pass each contribute, and the strongest signal wins.
  • Two guardrails prevent invented names. People who declined the invite are excluded, and a voice match is dropped if that person was not a calendar attendee for the meeting.
  • Renaming once teaches the app. A single rename updates the whole transcript, trains the voice fingerprint, and pre-seeds the name for the next meeting in a recurring series.
  • A quiet amber dot flags low confidence. It marks the labels worth a glance and disappears the moment you confirm or rename them.
  • Voice profiles attach to a stable identity. The same person is recognized across different email and name formats, while two people who share a first name at different companies stay separate.
Summaries, action items, and follow-ups

After every recording you get a summary, the action items, and a follow-up email draft you can send.

  • It writes a structured one-page summary. The summary follows your own editable prompt template, so it reads the way you want it to.
  • It extracts action items with owners and dates. Each item carries an assignee and a due date when one was stated, and you can push them to Apple Reminders.
  • It drafts the follow-up email. You get a subject and body ready to edit and send as soon as the call ends.
  • It gives you a clean transcript. A readable version sits alongside the raw, segment-by-segment transcript.
  • You can regenerate anything. Every output can be produced again at any time without re-recording the meeting.
Meeting preparation

Your prep is ready before the call starts, drawn from past conversations and your own documents.

  • A pre-meeting brief gathers the context. It collects who the attendees are, the most relevant past meetings, open action items, and agenda hints from the event description.
  • A Daily Brief covers your whole day. Every meeting in the day sits on one page with its prep notes, so a morning glance tells you where the day is going.
  • Related Meetings ranks your history. Past conversations are ordered by recurring series, shared attendees, and topical similarity.
  • Briefs compute ahead of time. The app prepares briefs in the background for meetings in the next 48 hours, so one is almost always ready when you open a meeting.
Search and Ask Anything

You can ask a question across every meeting you have ever recorded, and find any moment by keyword or by meaning.

  • Ask Anything spans all your meetings. It answers questions like what you decided about pricing last quarter, drawing on every recording plus your knowledge base.
  • ⌘K search covers everything you captured. It searches titles, transcripts, summaries, people, action items, and captured slides, by keyword and by semantic meaning.
  • Per-meeting chat goes deep on one call. You can interrogate a single conversation, including while it is still recording.
People and companies

Everyone you meet with becomes a profile that remembers your history and their voice.

  • A people directory holds each contact. Every person carries their aliases, voice fingerprint, organization, and full meeting history.
  • A companies view groups people by domain. Contacts are grouped by email domain, with cross-meeting rollups of open items and recent summaries.
  • You stay in control of identities. You can rename people, merge duplicates, edit aliases, and manage or delete voice fingerprints.
  • Contact import is opt-in and minimal. You can import names and emails from macOS Contacts and nothing else, with no phone numbers, photos, or notes.
Knowledge base

Point Local Meeting Manager at a folder of your documents and it uses them as context for prep and chat.

  • It indexes the formats you actually use. The app reads .md, .txt, .html, .docx, .pdf, and .eml files from a folder you choose and watches it for changes.
  • Your documents inform the answers. Retrieved passages feed the pre-meeting brief, the summary, and chat, so the AI knows your glossary, specs, and policies.
  • It can write notes back to the folder. An optional setting exports completed meeting notes as Markdown into the same folder, alongside your existing documents.
  • The index stays local. It is a local database, and nothing about it leaves your Mac unless a query pulls a passage into a cloud AI prompt.
Calendar integration

Connect Google or Apple Calendar so meetings, attendees, and RSVPs flow in automatically.

  • It works with both calendar systems. Google Calendar connects through read-only OAuth, and Apple Calendar comes through macOS EventKit, which also covers iCloud and Outlook for Mac.
  • You pick which calendars to include. Multi-calendar selection lets you choose exactly which calendars feed the app, for each provider.
  • Calendar data sharpens the rest of the app. It supplies speaker-attribution candidates, the expected number of voices, and the pre-meeting brief.
  • Access is read-only by design. The app reads your events but never writes to your calendar.
Analytics and insights

You can see how your meetings actually go, computed on your Mac from transcripts you already have.

  • It measures talk time and meeting load. You see counts, hours, talk share, busiest days, and trends, sliced by date range and participant.
  • It reads tone without an AI model. A coarse, neutral sentiment read per meeting and per speaker is computed entirely on-device.
  • It tracks the topics you define. Name a topic and the app counts how often it comes up across your meetings.
  • Your private speaking stats stay private. Your talk share, question rate, filler density, overlaps, and longest monologue are opt-in, never seen by any AI model, and hidden with one click.
  • It surfaces neutral relationship signals. Cues such as a contact going quiet, a cadence dropping, or action items aging are framed as signals to notice, not alerts.
  • It tells you whether a meeting paid off. State what you need beforehand, and afterward the app posts a neutral verdict on whether you got it, with decisions-per-hour and hit-rate rollups.
Beyond notes

The deeper capabilities go beyond notes, and each one is grounded strictly in your own recorded record.

  • Practice mode rehearses a hard conversation. A clearly labeled simulation argues only from a person's or company's recorded positions, cites each claim, and says so when the record does not cover something.
  • Handover documents brief whoever takes over. One click produces a six-section handover for a recurring series, built only from that series' recorded threads and summaries.
  • A glossary defines your jargon. The app mines recurring terms and defines them from how you actually used them in your meetings.
  • The knowledge gardener keeps the record consistent. A nightly pass links duplicate, superseded, and contradictory facts across meetings.
  • Slide capture makes on-screen text searchable. A one-click capture of the call window extracts text on-device with Apple's Vision OCR, and it captures nothing if it cannot confidently identify the call window.
  • Clips and key quotes mark the moments that matter. You can snapshot a verbatim quote and listen back, though sharing and export are intentionally not included today.
  • A transport bar syncs playback to the transcript. You can play the audio with tap-to-seek, a moving highlight on the current line, a follow mode, and adjustable speed.
Recipes and templates

You can bend the AI to your own way of working with reusable prompts and note structures.

  • Recipes run your own prompts on any meeting. Save a prompt such as turning the action items into Jira tickets or drafting three posts from the call, then re-run it whenever you like.
  • Meeting templates pre-fill the notepad. You can start from a structure such as a 1:1, a standup, or a planning session.
  • Prompt templates are yours to edit. The summary, the pre-meeting brief, and the follow-up email each use a template you can change, each with a reset to default.
Integrations

You can push action items to Apple Reminders and optionally enrich attendees from their email.

  • Apple Reminders receives your action items. You can push items into Reminders by hand or have the app do it automatically.
  • Apollo.io enrichment is strictly opt-in. If you add an Apollo key and turn it on, the app looks up an attendee's title and employer from their email address, and it never sends any meeting content.
